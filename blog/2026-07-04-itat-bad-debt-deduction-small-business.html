Bad Debt Deduction: ITAT Clarifies for Businesses

Ever wondered if a loan you gave for business or professional reasons, that went unpaid, could offer you a tax benefit? A recent ruling by the Ahmedabad Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) sheds light on exactly this scenario.

The Ahmedabad ITAT recently clarified that businesses can claim a bad debt deduction for amounts written off in their books, even if legal recovery efforts are still ongoing. As reported by Mint Money, this ruling allows the claim under specific statutory conditions, and importantly, it can also be treated as a business loss under Section 28 of the Income-tax Act, offering significant relief to entities facing such challenges.

While primarily for businesses, this ruling has implications for salaried professionals who might also have freelance income, run a small side business, or engage in professional services. If you've extended credit for your professional work or supplied goods/services on credit for a small venture that went unpaid, knowing these tax provisions can be crucial. It empowers you to understand potential tax relief when genuine business debts turn sour, preventing double losses.

If you operate a side hustle or provide professional services and extend credit, ensure proper documentation of all transactions. If a debt genuinely becomes irrecoverable, consult a tax advisor to understand the process of writing it off in your books and fulfilling statutory conditions. This proactive approach helps ensure you are well-positioned to claim legitimate deductions, like bad debt or business loss, should the need arise, optimizing your overall tax liability.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • Maintain meticulous records for all business-related credits and receivables, even for small freelance projects.
  • If a business debt appears irrecoverable, understand the process of writing it off in your accounting records as per statutory norms.
  • Consult a qualified tax professional to assess eligibility and procedures for claiming bad debt deduction or business loss under relevant IT Act sections.
